Every page of Baruch has the same **ribbon** at the top. It leads to everything Baruch can do – with the mouse,
with the keyboard and also without JavaScript.

## Ribbon {#menueband}

{{< hilfebild name="band-start" alt="The ribbon with the tab Start: in the first row the menu Baruch, the choice of work, title and author of the work and the five tabs; below them the groups View of the text, Text, Paragraph, Revision, Assistants and Ideas." titel="The ribbon on the work page, tab Start." >}}

**First row:** the menu **Baruch ▾**, on the work page the **choice of work**, the title of the page (on the work
page title and author of the work) and the **tabs**. **Second row:** the groups of the chosen tab; below each
group its name stands in small print.

### The five tabs {#reiter}

| Tab | Groups and entries |
|---|---|
| **Start** | Work page: *View of the text* (switch **Editor/Preview**), the buttons of the editor (*Text*, *Paragraph*, *Revision*, *Assistants*), *Ideas* (Capture idea · Ideas · Mind map). Other pages: *Work* (**To the text** – back to the work page), the groups of the page itself, *Ideas*. |
| **Insert** | Only on the work page: Footnote, Quotation, List, Enumeration, Term, Break, Link, Reference, the formats of the work template, Image, *Index and glossary*. |
| **Review** | Findings · Check quotations (Bible references) · Search and replace in the work · Check cross references · Literature · Sync. |
| **Book** | *Work* (Edit work · Outline · Outline requests, only when there are open ones · Outline scheme · Images · with accounts: Contributors) · *Typesetting* (Typeset preview · EPUB preview) · *Output* (PDF · Draft · EPUB · Note cards … · Word … · Export (Typst) · Exchange: Write out) · *Cover* (Cover · Cover PDF) · *Index and glossary* (Glossary · Index). |
| **View** | *Sidebars* (Outline, Assistants – only on the work page) · *Laptop mode* (Automatic · On · Off) · *Display* (theme: ◐ System → ☀ Light → ☾ Dark; Reset zoom). |

- **Which tabs there are:** the work page shows all five; pages belonging to a work show **Start · Review · Book ·
  View**; pages without a work **Start · View**.
- **Every page opens with its tab:** typesetting, cover, EPUB, exchange, glossary, index, images, outline scheme
  and "Edit work" with **Book**; findings, check quotations, search, cross references, literature and sync with
  **Review**; the settings with **View**; all others with **Start**.
- **Groups of the page itself:** pages outside the work page bring their own groups – **Back** (such as "Back to
  the work", "Back to the section", "All ideas"), **See also** (such as "Index" on the page of the glossary, "Mind
  map" on the list of ideas), **Works** ("New work or import…" and "Archive" in the list of works), **Download**
  and **Next** ("To the work" after reading in).
- **Which work is meant:** on the work page the one shown, on pages of a work that work, otherwise the one opened
  last. If there is none, the entries lead to the list of works ("Choose a work first"). On pages without a work,
  "Literature" and "Sync" stand under Start.
- **Badges** on the tabs show where something is waiting: on **Start** the number of open proposals in the whole
  work, on **Review** the open findings ("N open"), on **Book** the open outline requests. They appear on every
  page, also without JavaScript. Zero shows no badge.
- **Saving in the ribbon:** "Save" and "As new revision" in the group *Revision* do the same as the buttons of the
  status line below the sheet; without a change "Save" saves nothing. "Review" and "Proposal" in the group
  *Assistants* apply to the assistant chosen in the column on the right.

**Changing tabs with the keyboard:** ← and → move from tab to tab (at the end it continues at the front), Home and
End jump to the first or last; Tab leads from the chosen tab into its buttons. ← and → also move between the
buttons. All shortcuts are listed under [Keys and mouse](/en/hilfe/tasten/).

### Folding and unfolding the ribbon {#band-einklappen}

{{< hilfebild name="band-eingeklappt" alt="The work page with a folded ribbon: only the first row with the menu Baruch, the choice of work and the tabs is visible; the text has more space." titel="Folded, only the first row of the ribbon stays." >}}

How it works:

1. Click on the **chosen tab** or on **▴** at the far right of the first row. The ribbon folds; only the first row
   stays. Baruch remembers this.
2. In the folded ribbon a click on a tab shows its groups **temporarily** above the text. After a button has been
   pressed, with Esc or with a click beside them they disappear again.
3. **▾** on the right unfolds the ribbon permanently again.

### Compact ribbon {#kompaktes-band}

{{< hilfebild name="band-kompakt" alt="The compact ribbon in a window 1366 pixels wide: every group is a single button with a name and a small arrow; one group is open as a small window." titel="In the compact ribbon every group is one button." >}}

In laptop mode the ribbon is compact: every group is a button "Name ▾" that shows its entries as a small window.
Only one is open at a time; Esc or a click beside it closes it. In the first row the author is then left out, and
a long title is shortened with "…" so that the row stays a single line.

### Laptop mode {#laptop-modus}

{{< hilfebild name="band-laptop-arbeitsseite" alt="The work page in laptop mode at 1366 by 768 pixels: compact ribbon, on the left and right a narrow bar each instead of the columns, the text takes up almost the whole width." titel="Laptop mode: the text gets the space." >}}

On a small screen the text should get the space, not the controls. For this the laptop mode has three parts:

1. **Compact ribbon** (see above).
2. **Side columns as bars.** Outline (left) and assistants (right) fold into a narrow bar each at the edge. The
   text gets almost the whole width.
3. **Drawers.** A click on a bar brings its column over the text – as wide as needed, without squeezing the text
   – and puts it away again afterwards.

**Switching on:** in the tab **View**, group "Laptop mode", there are three switches. The pressed switch applies;
Baruch remembers the choice for one year in this browser, for all pages.

| Switch | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Automatic** (default) | Baruch goes by the width of the window. |
| **On** | Compact ribbon and bars, whatever the width of the window. |
| **Off** | Full ribbon and open columns, also in a narrow window. |

With **Automatic** the inner width of the browser window counts, on loading and on every change of size:

| Window width | Ribbon | Side columns |
|---|---|---|
| from 1600 pixels | full | open |
| 1280 to 1599 pixels | compact | open |
| below 1280 pixels | compact | bars |

A full-size window on a screen of 1920 × 1080 is therefore "full", one of 1366 × 768 "compact", half a window
"compact with bars". If you drag the window across a threshold, the view changes at once without the page
reloading; unsaved text stays. If you open Baruch in a window of another size than on the last visit, the page
appears in the fitting state straight away.

**Bars and drawers:**

- **Folding a column by hand:** "Collapse" at the top of the column, or **View → Sidebars → Outline** or
  **Assistants** (pressed means: column visible). The column becomes a bar and stays one until you open it
  again. The remembered column width is kept.
- **Bar in a wide window:** a click on the bar unfolds the column permanently again.
- **Bar in a narrow window** (below 1280 pixels or laptop mode "On"): the bar opens the column as a **drawer**
  above the text. It closes with Esc, with a click beside it, with "Close" at the top of the drawer – and by
  itself as soon as you choose a section in the outline. Afterwards the column is a bar again.
- The drawer of the assistants stays open as long as you work in it (chat, review, revisions).
- In a narrow window the columns cannot be placed permanently next to the text; for that, set the laptop mode to
  "Off" or widen the window.

In very narrow windows Baruch no longer stacks the columns one below the other; they become bars, and the text
keeps the full height.

## Zooming with Ctrl+mouse wheel {#vergroessern}

{{< hilfebild name="band-ansicht" alt="The tab View of the ribbon; in the group Display the button Reset zoom stands next to the choice of the theme." titel="Reset zoom sets all areas back to 100 %." >}}

**Ctrl+mouse wheel** (on the Mac Ctrl+mouse wheel as well; on the touchpad spreading two fingers) zooms in or out
**only the area below the mouse pointer** – never the whole page:

- the outline,
- in the editor, each on its own, the head, the sheet (typeset view, source text or preview) and the status line,
- the right column as a whole and within it, separately, the area "Revisions",
- an open window (literature, idea, import),
- on the other pages the content.

If areas lie inside one another, the innermost counts. Every area remembers its level, also across paging and
reloading. The columns keep their width; only font and content grow (50 % to 300 %, in steps of 10 %). Above the
ribbon and between the areas the gesture does nothing.

- **Ctrl+0 (Mac: ⌘0)** resets the area below the mouse pointer.
- **View → Reset zoom** sets all areas back to 100 % at once.
- The zoom of the browser through its menu (Ctrl++ and Ctrl+−, Mac: ⌘+ and ⌘−) stays untouched.
- Without JavaScript the browser zooms the whole page as usual.

## Help {#hilfe}

{{< hilfebild name="band-menue-baruch" alt="The opened menu Baruch; the entry Help stands directly above About Baruch." titel="The entry Help in the menu Baruch ▾." >}}

**Baruch ▾ → Help** opens this help on the website in a new window; for that the computer needs the Internet.

The entry is an ordinary link and works without JavaScript as well. The page
[About Baruch](/en/hilfe/erste-schritte/#ueber) also names the address of the help.

## Without JavaScript {#ohne-javascript}

{{< hilfebild name="band-ohne-javascript" alt="The work page without JavaScript: the ribbon shows tabs and groups as ordinary links, the sheet holds a text field with the source text, below it the buttons Save, Insert image and Marking." titel="Without JavaScript everything stays usable – with a text field instead of the typeset view." >}}

Without JavaScript everything stays reachable; each click then reloads the page.

- The menu Baruch ▾ and the groups are areas that can be unfolded, the tabs and all entries ordinary links and
  forms. Work, section and revision are kept when changing.
- The **buttons of the editor** (groups *Text*, *Paragraph*, *Revision*, *Assistants* and the whole tab
  **Insert**) need JavaScript. Without JavaScript you work in the text field and save with **Save** below the
  sheet; there you also find **Insert image …** and **Marking …** (for glossary and index).
- **Laptop mode:** without JavaScript Baruch does not know the width of the window. "Automatic" then always draws
  wide. On a small screen you set the laptop mode to **On** by hand.
- A bar opens its column as a drawer without JavaScript as well; "Close" or a click on the darkened area beside it
  puts it away again. Without JavaScript Esc does not close it.
- "Reset zoom" and zooming single areas exist only with JavaScript.
- **Known limit:** if a page is the answer to a submitted form (for example the question "Confirm saving"),
  a click on a tab without JavaScript leads away from this answer – it is no longer there afterwards.
